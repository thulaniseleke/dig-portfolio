Collaborative Learning / 04

Group Reflection

An examination of how groups develop, how collaboration influences learning, and what effective teamwork requires throughout a shared task.

Theoretical Framework

Tuckman's stages of group development

Bruce Tuckman's model describes five stages commonly associated with group development: Forming, Storming, Norming, Performing and Adjourning. The model provides a useful way to reflect on group communication, responsibilities, challenges and progress towards a shared objective. Groups do not always move through these stages in a perfectly linear way.

Stage 01

Forming

Forming is the initial stage in which group members become familiar with one another and begin understanding the task. Members may need time to clarify expectations, identify their strengths and decide how to work together.

Reflecting on this stage encourages me to recognise the importance of establishing a shared objective, discussing expectations and agreeing on how information will be communicated. Clear responsibilities can provide direction from the beginning.

Stage 02

Storming

During Storming, differences in opinions, approaches or expectations may become more visible. Members might disagree about priorities, the division of work or how a task should be completed.

This stage highlights the need to listen respectfully, explain viewpoints clearly and address disagreements constructively. Differences do not have to prevent progress when members focus on the shared task and remain willing to find workable solutions.

Stage 03

Norming

Norming involves developing stronger cooperation, clearer expectations and agreed ways of working. Members begin to understand their responsibilities and how their contributions connect to the group's overall objective.

This stage demonstrates the value of accountability, consistent communication and mutual respect. Agreeing on deadlines, sharing updates and supporting members who need assistance can help a group work more effectively.

Stage 04

Performing

At the Performing stage, a group is able to coordinate its work more effectively and concentrate on achieving its objectives. Members can use their different strengths to contribute to the task.

Effective performance depends on more than dividing work. Members also need to check the quality of their contributions, communicate progress and help resolve problems. This encourages me to view teamwork as a shared responsibility for the final outcome.

Stage 05

Adjourning

Adjourning occurs when the group completes its task and brings its work to a close. Members can review what they achieved, acknowledge contributions and identify lessons that can be applied in future collaborations.

Reflection at this stage is important because completing a task is not the only measure of learning. Reviewing the process can reveal what worked well, what could be improved and how individual members have developed.

Personal Learning

What effective collaboration requires

Group work provides an opportunity to learn from people with different ideas, experiences and approaches. A shared task requires members to communicate, manage their responsibilities and consider how their individual contributions affect the final result. It also requires flexibility because plans may need to change when challenges arise.

Communication and participation

Clear communication helps members understand what is expected and reduces the possibility of avoidable confusion. Listening is just as important as speaking because members need to understand different perspectives before making decisions. Meaningful participation also requires members to contribute rather than assuming that others will complete every task.

Responsibility and conflict management

A group works more effectively when members take ownership of their responsibilities and communicate honestly about progress. If disagreements arise, the focus should remain on understanding the problem and finding a solution. Respectful discussion, clear expectations and fair allocation of tasks can help protect working relationships while keeping the group focused on its objective.

Applying the lessons in future projects

In future group assignments, I want to contribute by understanding the task early, agreeing on responsibilities, keeping communication clear and completing my allocated work on time. I also want to be open to feedback and willing to assist where appropriate. These practices can strengthen collaboration and help ensure that the group produces work that reflects a shared effort.

Effective teamwork is built through shared responsibility, clear communication, respect and a commitment to a common goal.
